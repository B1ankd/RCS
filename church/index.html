Church Security Guide

A practical guide to church security

How to protect a house of worship while keeping it open, warm, and welcoming.

Start with a security assessment

Good security starts with an honest look at your building and grounds. A church security assessment walks your campus the way a visitor, or an attacker, would: the parking lot, every entrance, the lobby, children's areas, offices, and the sanctuary. The goal is a short list of the gaps that matter most, ranked by risk, not a hundred-page report nobody reads.

Build a church safety team

Most incidents are noticed before they happen. A trained safety team, made up of volunteers who know what to watch for, is the single most effective step most churches can take. We train greeters and safety volunteers to read behavior, cover entrances and the parking lot, and respond calmly when something feels wrong.

Control your entrances

You cannot protect a space you cannot see or enter. Reduce the number of unlocked doors during services, keep a clear line of sight to the main entrance, and lock down children's areas with a simple check-in and check-out system. None of this has to feel cold; a friendly greeter at a single main door is both hospitality and security.

Plan for a medical or violent emergency

The worst day is not the time to invent a plan. Write down who calls 911, who leads an evacuation, where people go, and how you account for children. Then practice it. A one-page plan that everyone knows beats a detailed binder that sits on a shelf.

Protect against insider risk

Churches run on trust, which is exactly what a bad actor relies on. Simple steps, background checks for anyone working with children, two-adult rules, and clear handling of money and keys, close the gaps that trust alone leaves open.

Want this tailored to your building? We will walk your campus and hand you a ranked, plain-language plan your team can actually follow.
